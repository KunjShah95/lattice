Teams instrument their LLM systems, then discover during their first incident
that they cannot answer the only question that mattered: what did the model
actually receive, and what did it actually return?

The reason is usually that they logged what is easy — status, latency, token
count — rather than what a question requires.

## What a trace has to contain

For a trace to be useful six weeks later, it has to be sufficient to
reconstruct the decision. That means capturing, on every request:

**The full input.** The complete system prompt and user message as sent, not a
truncated preview. Truncation is where traces become useless, and it is usually
done for cost reasons that are dwarfed by the value of being able to debug.

**The full output**, plus the stop reason. A truncated response and a refusal
look identical in a metrics dashboard and completely different in an incident.

**The resolved model identity**, including the version. "gpt-4" is not an
identifier; the specific snapshot is. Model aliases move underneath you and
this is the single most common reason a bug cannot be reproduced.

**Per-hop detail.** In a multi-step system, which steps ran, in what order,
what each returned, and what each cost. A trace that only records the final
answer records the least useful part.

**Parameters that were not defaults.** Temperature, top-p, the schema if one
was enforced, the retrieval results if retrieval ran. These are the things
that change behaviour and that nobody remembers.

**Outcome signals.** Validation passed or failed, which route was taken, cache
hit or miss, user feedback, downstream business result if you have one. These
are what turn a trace log into the raw material of an
[eval set](/blog/evals-are-the-asset).

## What to leave out

**Secrets and regulated data.** Redact at write time, not at read time. A trace
store holding unredacted PII is a liability that grows with every request, and
you cannot retroactively redact what you already logged.

**Full verbosity on high-volume paths.** Sample. A trace at 100% on a
high-traffic system can cost real money and rarely changes a decision — because
the interesting requests are rare by definition. Sample the boring ones
aggressively and keep everything that errored, retried or drew feedback.

**Derived state you can recompute.** If a token count can be recomputed from
the text, do not store both.

## Emit one standard, not four

The failure mode of home-grown instrumentation is schema drift. Every service
logs slightly different fields, and the cross-service question — "did this slow
request also cost more?" — becomes a manual join nobody performs.

Adopt OpenTelemetry as your export format even if you use a proprietary
collector. It costs almost nothing to emit and it is the only way your traces
survive a change of vendor. Every platform in
[Evaluation & Observability](/evaluation-observability) that is worth using
speaks it.

## Make the trace answerable from the UI

A trace you have to export and query with hand-written SQL is a trace nobody
will use during an incident. The properties that matter:

**Filter by any field.** Identity, route, model, outcome. This is what turns
"LLM requests are slow" into "agent loop step three is slow for tenants over
a certain size".

**Show the diff between two runs.** The most common debugging question is not
"what happened" but "what changed". If you can put a failed trace next to a
successful one from last week, you will solve most incidents in minutes.

**Link a trace to a cost and a latency figure per hop.** Not aggregate —
per hop. Aggregate cost tells you where to look; per-hop cost tells you what
to change.

## Retention is a product decision

Set it deliberately. Full-fidelity traces for thirty days, sampled metadata
for a year, outcomes forever. The reason outcomes deserve the longest life is
that they are small and they are the only thing that lets you compare a change
from eighteen months ago against today.

The [Evaluation & Observability](/evaluation-observability) section covers the
tooling. If you want the discipline rather than the tools, start with
[evals are the asset](/blog/evals-are-the-asset) — because a trace with no
outcomes attached is a very expensive log file.
